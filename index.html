How the numbers & trust signals work

How the numbers work: ₹/36 g = selling price ÷ net weight × 36. Protein and BCAA per 36 g are scaled from the listed scoop size; where Amazon gives no scoop size it is derived from net weight ÷ servings (marked *). "Protein per ₹100" = protein per 36 g ÷ ₹/36 g × 100, the clearest value-for-money figure. Protein type is inferred from the title and ingredients. Outliers are sachets, trial packs and imports priced over ₹400 or under ₹15 per scoop. Prices drift daily; check the Amazon link before buying.

How the trust signals work: Lab-test claim means the listing cites a third-party test (Trustified, Labdoor, Informed Choice/Sport or similar). That is the seller's claim, not something verified here. Too cheap? marks dairy proteins giving over 60 g of protein per ₹100, far above the rest of the market. Added aminos means the ingredients list free glycine, taurine, glutamine or arginine, which some brands use to inflate protein figures ("amino spiking"). Protein % high marks a label claiming over 92% protein per serving, and Unbranded means no brand stands behind the listing. The label dots (●●●●) count how many of these the listing states: protein, serving size, BCAA and ingredients. Treat red flags as prompts to check reviews or a lab report, not as verdicts.

Label serving vs 36 g: "label serving" is the scoop size the brand prints, which ranges from about 25 g to 50 g. Every "/ 36 g" column rescales to one standard scoop so products compare fairly.
